# Observer Cam architecture

## Observer entity

`ObserverCameraEntity` is a 0.96 × 0.96 block-sized, no-gravity entity registered on both logical sides. It is invulnerable, non-pickable, non-pushable, ignores block triggers, emits no movement events, and has no loot table. The server owns its owner, target, movement, and shot state. Owner and target are deliberately separate so retargeting a camera cannot transfer lifecycle control to another player. Both identities, follow state, and compact HUD diagnostics are synchronized and persisted.

The client renderer submits Minecraft's existing `Blocks.OBSERVER` moving-block model. No Observer texture or model is duplicated. The canonical model faces south (+Z); the entity's interpolated yaw and pitch rotate the entire cube so the observing face follows the camera forward vector.

## Authoritative camera transform

`CameraTransform` is the only place that defines Observer camera geometry. It interpolates the physical block center, calculates Minecraft's yaw/pitch forward vector, and moves the origin 0.515 blocks outside the observing face. `CameraMixin` applies that origin after vanilla camera setup. `ObserverCameraSmoother` then applies a render-rate, time-based low-pass filter to the base position and wrapped yaw/pitch, removing visible 20 TPS stepping without changing the server-authoritative entity. Large teleports and long frame gaps reset the filter immediately. The POV origin and view direction come from the same smoothed transform, keeping the client camera internally aligned.

`ObserverPovController` sets the Observer as Minecraft's camera entity while leaving `Minecraft.player` unchanged. `LocalPlayerMixin` keeps the real player recognized as the controlled camera owner, preserving movement, mining, placement, inventory, and interaction input. `LevelRendererMixin` explicitly extracts the local player's render state while Observer POV is active and suppresses only its floating name tag. The controller remembers the previous perspective and restores it whenever POV is toggled off, the Observer becomes invalid, or the connection closes.

## Planning and scoring

`CameraDirector` runs `CandidateGenerator` every four ticks (five plans per second). `DocumentaryFraming` maintains horizontal and vertical soft zones, damps jump response, and bounds changes to the planning heading. Candidate generation predicts a few horizontal player-velocity ticks ahead, estimates enclosure, blends indoor/outdoor distance, and creates 18 side/front/rear/elevated positions without touching unloaded chunks. It considers at most four nearby visible living subjects, modestly shifts and widens the group composition, and always keeps the tracked player weighted as the primary subject.

Each candidate is evaluated by:

1. `VisibilityProbe` — four collision raycasts at head, torso, waist, and leg heights. Up to two leaf blocks may be crossed before a sample is considered hidden.
2. destination AABB clearance and a direct movement-path ray, which is required during normal flight;
3. distance and projected framing for the selected camera FOV;
4. `EnvironmentProbe` — sky, ceiling, nearby wall, and five background-depth rays;
5. `ShotScorer` — visibility-first weighted scoring with continuity, front-shot policy, and a bounded bonus for including nearby secondary subjects.

The director maintains a selected angle. With default stability it holds an angle for roughly nine seconds before accepting a meaningfully better shot, and after roughly 15–18 seconds it may introduce a comparable alternate view. Between the five planning passes per second, the held camera destination and framing focus translate with the target's horizontal motion; this prevents sideways movement from aiming at a stale four-tick position without turning ordinary jumps into camera bob. Immediate side changes are reserved for an invalid path or serious visibility failure. Low-scoring but valid shots first reframe within the held angle. If no reachable candidate exists, the Observer holds instead of continuing toward a stale blocked destination. Persistent zero visibility or stalled movement waits roughly two seconds before promoting a clear, visible position to a recovery teleport; compact low-height candidates allow this recovery inside two-block tunnels and around sharp corners. During normal movement, nine body-width path probes prevent the block-sized camera from clipping a corner that only its center ray could clear. The small state set is `HOLD`, `REFRAME`, `REPOSITION`, `CATCH_UP`, `RECOVER_LOS`, and `EMERGENCY_RECOVERY`.

## Motion

`MotionMath` produces a velocity target from position error, then applies acceleration and speed limits. `MotionController` moves through Minecraft collision resolution each tick and damps the actual resulting velocity. Rotation uses wrapped yaw/pitch error, bounded large-error catch-up, immediate braking when the subject crosses sides, and an overshoot clamp that removes the visible snap-back kink. Separation beyond the emergency threshold permits one teleport to the planned clear destination.

## Client systems

- `ObserverCameraRenderer` renders the vanilla Observer block.
- `ObserverPovController` owns safe POV entry and restoration.
- `GameRendererMixin` applies configured FOV only while the Observer is the camera and performs interaction picking from the real player's eyes and look direction, so mining, placement, attacks, and use remain server-valid in detached POV.
- `LocalPlayerMixin` preserves normal player control while viewing through the Observer.
- `LevelRendererMixin` keeps the local player visible in the detached POV without a name tag.
- `ObserverDebugHud` renders compact synchronized shot diagnostics.
- `ObserverDebugRenderer` recomputes the low-rate client plan and visualizes it with optional particles.
- `ObserverPictureInPicture` owns the shared auxiliary Observer render used by PiP, background recording, and background replay. It refreshes camera uniforms on both sides of the temporary camera switch, keeps one asynchronous readback in flight, and releases its render target and texture on the render thread. PiP is off by default and exposes Small/Medium/Big display presets plus bounded resolution, 2–60 FPS, and 25–100% opacity controls. Big preserves the original responsive width; the smaller presets scale only the HUD presentation and do not increase render cost. Compact is the default frame style and leaves only a one-pixel outline; Labeled optionally adds the 11-pixel status header.
- `ObserverCamConfigScreen` provides lightweight built-in pages; `ObserverCamModMenu` exposes them through optional Mod Menu integration.

The Mod Menu landing page exposes cameraman, POV, recording, PiP, and output-folder quick actions. A short spawn-sync wait is shown as **Cancel POV Request** rather than looking like a failed click. Every Minecraft shortcut is unbound by default, while the settings screens keep every action usable without a shortcut. The enabled preference and a bounded camera-settings snapshot are sent to the server. Runtime settings are isolated by owner UUID and validated against the same limits as the UI, so dedicated-server directors honor live settings without one player changing another player's camera. The server retains exactly one Observer owned by the requesting player across all dimensions and removes that owner's stale duplicates without touching cameras merely targeted at them. Disabling or disconnecting removes the owner's camera wherever it is and restores normal POV.

The optional assistant remains client-only. Its scheduler counts active gameplay time, a 39-entry translation-backed catalogue prevents immediate repeats, and at most one eight-active-gameplay-second utterance is active. The existing Observer renderer submits the short caption through Minecraft's depth-tested name-tag path only for the local player's owned Observer; it is suppressed and paused in menus, and hidden with the HUD, in full POV, in auxiliary capture passes, and beyond 32 blocks. Full facts can be copied directly to local chat and optionally passed once to Minecraft's bundled non-spatial narrator. No assistant state or speech crosses the network.

Observer celebrations are deliberately separate from the camera director. The server checks a three-block cube for a genuinely playing `JukeboxBlockEntity` twice per second and synchronizes one dancing flag. While dancing it sends a bounded note particle every eight ticks; the client renderer adds only a visual bob, sway, and occasional full turn, so neither entity motion nor POV geometry changes. `ObserverCelebrations` observes cake use without consuming or cancelling the interaction, verifies the cake's bite state at the end of the server tick, then starts one 440-tick celebration. The 22-second cake sound cannot overlap itself. Observer enable/disable sounds are emitted by the authoritative server transition; recording feedback is a single local UI sound after the encoder actually enters or leaves recording.

## Recording pipeline

`ObserverRecordingManager` owns a four-state recording session: idle, starting, recording, and finalizing. Recording requires the player's owned Observer but not full Observer POV. In normal player view, the shared auxiliary render produces a clean Observer feed at the selected output size and rate; in Observer POV, the main render is captured directly. Recording stops safely on Observer/world loss, framebuffer resize, shutdown, encoder failure, low free space, or the configured folder cap. A session token rejects late asynchronous callbacks from older recordings.

Starting recording, replay buffering, or PiP sends a narrow activation signal to the server-owned camera. The entity uses the vanilla Observer block's powered model for exactly two game ticks, producing the familiar brief red output-dot pulse without a custom glowing-eye overlay or a permanent powered state.

`GameRendererMixin` requests a frame immediately before Minecraft renders its GUI for the default clean-world output, or at the end of the render for optional HUD inclusion. `ObserverFrameCapture` delegates GPU readback to Minecraft's asynchronous screenshot path, converts the resulting ABGR pixels to RGBA, and closes the native image immediately. At most one readback may be in flight.

`FFmpegEncoder` accepts frames through a three-frame bounded queue. Its dedicated writer thread owns FFmpeg's standard input, so neither GPU completion callbacks nor the render thread can wait for encoding. `FFmpegCommand` supplies explicit raw-video dimensions, pixel format, and frame rate and maps the three supported formats to fixed safe codec options. `RecordingResolution` supplies current-window and aspect-preserving 720p, 1080p, 1440p, and 4K filters; `RecordingQuality` maps the three human-readable quality levels to bounded codec-appropriate CRF values. Clean sessions move a unique `.partial` output to its final name; failures retain the partial output and an FFmpeg log. `RecordingStorageBudget` measures the selected directory without loading file contents, enforcing the 3 GB default cap throughout capture. Finalization can repeat at most one second of the final accepted frame to preserve the intended timeline after small queue drops without creating an unbounded freeze frame.

The optional instant replay mode reuses the same capture coordinator and never runs a second encoder beside a normal recording. `ReplayBufferEncoder` asks FFmpeg for two-second, keyframe-aligned segments and keeps its three-frame input queue bounded. `ReplayBufferPolicy` chooses only oldest completed segments for time-, size-, or global-headroom eviction; the newest segment is treated as active and is never selected. Temporary sessions are direct children of `.observercam-replay-buffer` and require an ownership marker before cleanup code can remove them. `ReplayExporter` stops the active segment, writes a generated-name-only concat manifest, and stream-copies retained segments to the selected final format without a second quality loss. A successful export removes the private session; a failure keeps it with diagnostics. Buffering then resumes if the setting is enabled and the owned Observer remains available.

Optional audio uses an explicitly selected Windows DirectShow loopback input in the same FFmpeg process and clock as the raw video pipe. MP4/MKV encode AAC, WebM encodes Opus, and instant-replay segments/export preserve the matching stream. The setting is off by default, and the device picker filters out ordinary microphones. Observer Cam does not install a virtual driver or replace Minecraft's live OpenAL device with `ALC_SOFT_loopback`; doing so would require taking ownership of sound mixing and could break sound-engine, HRTF, and voice-chat mods.

## Optional assistant

`ObserverAssistant` is a client-only, opt-in personality layer. It requires the local player's owned Observer, writes only to local chat, and never sends assistant text over networking. `AssistantFactScheduler` is pure timing policy with a validated one-to-five-minute interval; enabling or re-enabling starts a fresh interval rather than producing an immediate message. `AssistantCaptionTimer` gives each preview or scheduled caption eight active-gameplay seconds even when it began inside Mod Menu. `AssistantFactCatalog` is an isolated translation-backed source that avoids immediate repeats. Future assistant behaviors can be added beside the fact source without coupling them to camera direction, entity authority, or recording.

## Performance boundaries

Planning is fixed at five times per second; motion and safety checks run once per game tick; vanilla entity interpolation is followed by a lightweight client-only render-rate camera filter. Candidate probes only examine loaded chunks around the target. No pathfinder, chunk loader, or image analysis is present. Recording work is bounded to one GPU readback, three queued frames, and one FFmpeg writer thread per active live or replay session. Optional PiP and background capture perform one additional world render at their configured bounded size and rate and allow only one asynchronous capture in flight. Assistant timing is a constant-time client tick check with no network traffic. Celebration discovery checks at most 343 nearby block positions twice per second per Observer and emits at most one dance particle every eight ticks.
